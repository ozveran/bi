Early Life and Education
Napoleone di Buonaparte was born on the island of Corsica, one year after it passed from Genoa to France. The
son
of
a
minor
noble
family,
he
won
a
scholarship
to
French
military
schools
and
graduated
from
the
École
Militaire in Paris in 1785, commissioned as an artillery officer. A gifted student of mathematics, history and
tactics, he was also an avid reader who wrote prolifically in his youth.
Rise During the Revolution
The
French
Revolution
opened
careers
to
talent.
In
1793
Napoleon's
artillery
plan
helped
retake
the
port
of
Toulon from British-backed forces, earning him promotion to brigadier general at just 24. After he crushed a
royalist uprising in Paris in 1795, he was given command of the Army of Italy, where his campaigns of 1796–97
defeated Austria and made him a national hero. His Egyptian expedition (1798–99) ended in military failure, but
his image at home survived.
Seizing Power
Returning
to
France,
Napoleon
took
part
in
the
coup
of
18
Brumaire
(November
1799)
that
overthrew
the
Directory.
As
First
Consul
he
stabilized
the
country,
reformed
finance,
founded
the
Bank
of
France,
and
reorganized
the
state.
In
1804
he
crowned
himself
Emperor
of
the
French
in
Notre-Dame,
confirmed
by
a
plebiscite.
Legacy of Reform
His
most
lasting
achievement
was
the
Napoleonic
Code
(1804),
which
established
legal
equality,
protected
property and codified civil law. He also centralized administration through prefects, created the lycée system and
the Legion of Honour, and signed the Concordat of 1801 to end the conflict with the Catholic Church. Many of
these institutions still shape France and influenced legal systems worldwide.
Wars and Empire
Between 1803 and 1815 the Napoleonic Wars reshaped Europe. Victories at Austerlitz (1805), Jena (1806) and
Wagram
(1809)
made
France
the
dominant
continental
power,
while
the
Continental
System
tried
to
cripple
Britain
economically.
The
empire
overreached:
the
Peninsular
War
in
Spain
drained
resources,
and
the
1812
invasion of Russia destroyed most of his Grande Armée. Defeated at Leipzig in 1813, he abdicated in April 1814
and was exiled to Elba.
Hundred Days and Exile
In March 1815 Napoleon escaped Elba, reclaimed power, and marched on Paris. His final campaign ended at
Waterloo on 18 June 1815 against Wellington and Blücher. Exiled by the British to remote Saint Helena in the
South Atlantic, he dictated memoirs that shaped his own legend and died there in 1821, aged 51 (stomach cancer
is the most widely accepted cause).
Assessment
Napoleon remains one of history's most debated figures: a brilliant strategist and administrator who spread legal
and
administrative
modernization,
yet
an
authoritarian
ruler
whose
ambitions
cost
millions
of
lives,
restored
slavery in the French colonies in 1802, and curtailed press freedom. Both the reformer and the conqueror are part
of his legacy.
